Snapshot of Oct 11, 2026

Find DoD zero trust work in public federal records.

Policy to Pipeline Radar reads public federal records, sorts them by how strong the evidence is, and adds an AI read of how relevant each one is to Department of Defense zero trust work. Every call shows its reason, how often three AI reads agreed, and a link to the source.

Built for capture managers, the people at a federal contractor who decide which bids are worth chasing. The goal is to spot likely work before a solicitation posts. This prototype covers the first step: finding the signals and explaining each one.

A course prototype for MPIM 6750, Developing and Managing AI Products, Georgetown University.

  1. Pull public recordsFederal Register rules and notices, USAspending awards, and SAM.gov opportunities. Nothing internal or paid.
  2. Rules assign an evidence tierPlain rules rank each record by the type of evidence it is, and the rule is written next to it. No AI here.
  3. AI reads for relevance, three timesClaude calls each record High, Medium, Low, or Unclear three separate times. Confidence is how often the reads agree.
  4. You make the callOpen any signal for the source text and every AI read, and flag anything wrong. Nothing changes on its own.

When the rule and the AI disagree, the Radar says so.

A tier measures what kind of evidence a record is. It says nothing about whether the record matters to zero trust work. The two FAR overhaul rules are the clearest case: proposed rules sit in the strongest tier, yet all three AI reads rated them Low. Ranking them first would mislead a capture manager, so the Radar flags them for a person instead.

How much to trust the AI read

Confidence and accuracy answer different questions. Confidence is how often the AI agreed with itself. Accuracy is how often it agreed with a person who knows the domain. This section shows both, so you can see whether high confidence earns trust.

How it works, and what it cannot do

Rules and AI stay in separate lanes, so a reader can always tell which one made a call.

Evidence tiers, set by rules

  • Tier 1: final rules, proposed rules, and presidential documents.
  • Tier 2: Federal Register notices, contract awards, and SAM.gov notices.
  • Tier 3: everything else.
  • A tier measures the type of evidence, not relevance. That gap is why the AI read exists.

The AI relevance read

"For each signal, read the title, agency, and text and judge how relevant it is to Department of Defense zero trust work: High, Medium, or Low. Give a one-sentence reason that names the words in the record that drove the call. If the record is too thin to judge, answer Unclear instead of guessing. Use nothing outside the record, and judge the work described, not the company named."
  • Run three times, independently, on Oct 22, 2026, with Claude (Anthropic). The reads are saved with the data, so this page makes no live AI calls.
  • Confidence comes from agreement across the three runs, not from the model rating itself.
  • The AI never changes a tier, and a disagreement never changes the AI read on its own.

Known limits

  • One snapshot, Oct 11, 2026. The notebook pulls fresh data; this page does not.
  • SAM.gov searches titles only and returned no DoD matches that day.
  • DoD award data reaches USAspending on a 90-day delay, so recent awards are missing.
  • Accuracy is measured against one analyst's labels, and that check was not fully blind (see the method note). It is a first look, not a validated rate.
  • Your disagree flags stay in your own browser. In the product they would go to a review log.
  • Lead-time estimates and the capture brief drafter from the design are not built yet.

Source and build

  • Public sources only: the Federal Register API, the USAspending API, and the SAM.gov Get Opportunities API.
  • Pipeline, rules, and data live in the GitHub repository (a Jupyter notebook in Python).
  • Built by prompting Claude (Anthropic). Every prompt is in PROMPTS.md.
  • Company names come from public award records. The AI read judges the work described, not the company.
