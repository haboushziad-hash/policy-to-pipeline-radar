Policy to Pipeline Radar reads public federal records, sorts them by how strong the evidence is, and adds an AI read of how relevant each one is to Department of Defense zero trust work. Every call shows its reason, how confident the AI is, and a link to the source.

Built for capture managers: the people at a federal contractor who decide which bids are worth chasing.

STEP 1

Pull public records

Federal Register rules and notices, USAspending contract awards, and SAM.gov opportunities. Nothing internal or paid.

STEP 2 · RULES, NO AI

Assign an evidence tier

Plain rules place each record on a three-tier ladder by the type of evidence it is, with the rule written next to it.

STEP 3 · AI

Read for relevance, three times

Claude reads each record three separate times and calls it High, Medium, Low, or Unclear. Confidence is how often the three reads agree.

STEP 4 · YOU

Make the call

Open any signal to see the source text and every AI read. Flag anything wrong. Nothing changes on its own.

How much to trust the AI read

Confidence and accuracy are different questions. Confidence says how often the AI agreed with itself. Accuracy says how often it agreed with a person who knows the domain. This section shows both, so you can see whether high confidence actually earns trust.

How it works, and what it cannot do

The Radar keeps rules and AI in separate lanes, so a reader can always tell which one made a call.

Evidence tiers (rules)

  • Tier 1: final rules, proposed rules, and presidential documents.
  • Tier 2: Federal Register notices, contract awards, and SAM.gov notices.
  • Tier 3: everything else.
  • A tier measures the type of evidence, not relevance. That gap is why the AI read exists.

AI relevance read

"For each signal, read the title, agency, and text and judge how relevant it is to Department of Defense zero trust work: High, Medium, or Low. Give a one-sentence reason that names the words in the record that drove the call. If the record is too thin to judge, answer Unclear instead of guessing. Use nothing outside the record, and judge the work described, not the company named."

  • Run three times, independently, on Oct 22, 2026, with Claude (Anthropic). The reads are saved with the data, so this page makes no live AI calls.
  • Confidence comes from agreement across the three runs, not from the model rating itself.
  • The AI never changes a tier, and a disagreement never changes the AI read on its own.

Known limits

  • One snapshot (Oct 11, 2026). The notebook pulls fresh data; this page does not.
  • SAM.gov searches titles only and returned no DoD matches that day.
  • DoD award data reaches USAspending on a 90-day delay, so recent awards are missing.
  • Accuracy is measured against one analyst's labels on 35 records. That is a first check, not a validated rate.
  • Your disagree flags stay in your own browser. In the product they would go to a review log.
  • Lead-time estimates and the capture brief drafter from the design are not built yet.

Source and build

  • Public sources only: Federal Register API, USAspending API, SAM.gov Get Opportunities API.
  • Pipeline, rules, and data: GitHub repository (Jupyter notebook, Python).
  • Built by prompting Claude (Anthropic); every prompt is in PROMPTS.md.
  • Company names come from public award records. The AI read judges the work described, not the company.
